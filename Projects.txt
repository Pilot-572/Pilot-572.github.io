1. DIY E-Ink Tablet:
	1.1 A custom-built, DIY e-ink tablet with a pen and/or touchscreen
	1.2 A custom e-ink tablet built from scratch with the aim of not having heavy school textbooks and notebooks for every subject I have in that day. Of course, I can't eliminate all of the notebooks and textbooks with the sole reason being that some teachers require them, but I can certainly use them at most of the subjects I have. I want to make it to be accessible but still look and feel like a quality product, because no parent or kid is going to pay, for example, for a Remarkable product (lovely products but expensive). I can say that with this project I could go at national competitions, for example ONCS or RoSEF.

2. Sim Racing Rig:
	2.1 My own realistic sim racing rig on which I can be even more competitive.
	2.2 I currently have built a custom force feedback direct drive wheelbase with a steering wheel which I made myself. I used the FFBeast firmware and software but I can say it was a real job documenting myself and  finding the compatible parts for it. Several builds are already in the planning stage, one of them being a from-scratch aluminium extrusion frame designed and assembled by me, with the direction of having a rigid frame to have a driving position which is fixed, so the wheel, pedals and monitor sit exactly the same every session, and muscle memory can actually build. No flex, no shift, no guessing where the brake point is because the pedals moved. With it I want to continue my Iracing career, now currently being on Assetto Corsa Competizione.

3. Home NAS Server:
	3.1 TrueNAS on a2008 Phenom, RAIDZ1, ran on less than 800 gb of storage across 4 drives. Ran it, learned from it, retired it.
	3.2 A repurposed old PC into a NAS server with cross-device file sync, but the hardware was simply too old and the storage too small for it to be a practical project to keep running. I can, of course, upgrade the storage and use it but the recent prices of RAM and storage make it just too expensive. I can confidently say that with this project I learned home networking, Linux configuration and how to build something useful out of hardware most people would just throw away.
